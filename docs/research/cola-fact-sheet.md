# COLA / COLAs Online — 100-Point Fact Sheet

Compiled 2026-07-31 from primary TTB sources: the [COLAs and Formulas Online FAQs (print version)](https://www.ttb.gov/faqs/colas-and-formulas-online-faqs/print) (Q&A C1-C39, followed in full), the [Allowable Changes to Approved Labels page](https://www.ttb.gov/regulated-commodities/labeling/allowable-revisions), [TTB label processing times](https://www.ttb.gov/regulated-commodities/labeling/processing-times), and TTB Form 5100.31. FAQ item numbers (C1-C39) cited where applicable.

## A. What a COLA is (legal frame)

1. COLA = Certificate of Label Approval, required before an alcohol beverage label enters U.S. commerce, applied for on **TTB Form 5100.31**, "Application For And Certification/Exemption of Label/Bottle Approval" (C1).
2. The same form and system also cover a **certificate of exemption** from label approval (for products not entering interstate commerce) (C1).
3. It additionally covers **distinctive liquor bottle approval** — a third approval type most people never encounter (C1).
4. The reviewing office is TTB's **Alcohol Labeling and Formulation Division (ALFD)** (C1).
5. Labeling rules live in **27 CFR Part 4 (wine), Part 5 (distilled spirits), Part 7 (malt beverages), and Part 16 (health warning statement)** (allowable-revisions page).
6. Confidentiality of *pending* applications is governed by regulation — **27 CFR 13.61** (C2).
7. There is **no application fee** for COLA applications or formula submissions (C39).
8. Electronic filing is **optional** — paper Form 5100.31 is still accepted (C7).
9. The FAQ's system-facing content was last reviewed/updated around **July 10, 2020** for imaging questions — the guidance itself is a stability signal (C28-C35 datelines).
10. An approved e-filed COLA carries the statement **"Application was e-filed"** in the applicant signature box plus a **facsimile of the TTB approving official's signature** (C27).

## B. COLAs Online (the system)

11. COLAs Online launched as TTB's internet filing system in **2003** (prior research; system-of-record since).
12. It is simultaneously ALFD's **sole internal database** — tracking *all* COLA work, paper and electronic (C1).
13. Approved COLAs are published in the **Public COLA Registry**, a no-login public module (C1, C2).
14. The registry includes **printable versions** of approvals where available (C2).
15. The registry retains approvals that later **expired, were surrendered, or revoked** — it is a historical record, not just a current list (C2).
16. Registry URL: `ttbonline.gov/colasonline/publicSearchColasBasic.do` (C26).
17. There is a **48-hour delay** before approved COLAs post publicly (C26).
18. Paper submissions **cannot be tracked** in COLAs Online by applicants (C1, C2).
19. E-filers get an **immediate electronic receipt confirmation** and a **unique TTB ID** for tracking (C3).
20. Built-in **business rules block submission** of applications with certain incorrect or incomplete information (C3).
21. TTB's own stated caveat on automation: consistency "can be addressed **only to a limited degree** by a Web-based system… the human element remains" (C5).
22. The system is available **22 hours per day, 7 days a week** (C3, C14).
23. Daily maintenance window: **4:00-6:00 AM Eastern** (C14).
24. TTB explicitly frames COLAs Online as an *option* that does not reduce access to human specialists (C6).
25. States and Customs (USCBP) are **not electronically notified** of approvals — they use the public registry; auto-notification is listed as a *future enhancement* (C26).

## C. Registration and access

26. Two registration paths: **electronic** (Register for TTB Online) or **paper** (TTB Form 5013.2, COLAs Online Access Request) (C8).
27. Paper registration requires **original signatures**, mailed to ALFD at 1310 G Street NW, Box 12, Washington DC 20005 (C8).
28. Filers without inherent authority need a **Signing Authority form (TTB F 5100.1)** or **Power of Attorney (TTB F 5000.8)** on file *before* registering (C8).
29. Those authority forms are processed by the **TTB National Revenue Center (NRC)**, a different office than ALFD (C8).
30. **Third-party filers are explicitly permitted** — trade associations, law firms, consultants — the regulatory basis of the commercial filing market (C11).
31. Submitting an eApplication **legally equates to signing** the COLA form (C11).
32. Third-party filers need signature authority or POA **per company** they file for (C11).
33. Paper registrations get **no acknowledgement of receipt**; electronic ones get a submission ID (C10).
34. Registration processing times are published on TTB.gov so applicants needn't call (C10, C38).
35. Account sharing is **prohibited under any circumstances** for security; departing employees' accounts must be deleted via phone or form (C16).
36. A registered user moving companies must have the old permit **removed from their account** (C16).
37. ALFD's help line: **1-866-927-ALFD (2533)**, weekdays 8:00 AM-4:30 PM Eastern (C6).
38. In-person or scheduled meetings with **label specialists or supervisors** remain available to industry (C6).

## D. Technical requirements (a period piece)

39. Officially supported browsers: **Safari 5.1+, Chrome 21+, Internet Explorer 8+, Firefox 15+** (C12).
40. TTB's stated recommendation is **Internet Explorer 8+ on Windows**, with "compatibility mode" advised for others (C12) — a measure of the system's age.
41. An **active email account is required** — all status notifications flow by email (C13).
42. TTB anticipates users may need **a scanner** and image software to convert PDFs to JPEG/PNG (C13).
43. Passwords expire every **120 days** (C19).
44. Password complexity: ≥8 chars, upper+lower+numeral+special, must not contain the user ID (C20).
45. **Banned password characters**: single-quote, double-quote, underscore, equals, ampersand, at-sign (C20) — a signature of legacy input sanitization.
46. No reuse of the **last 10 passwords** or any password used within **48 hours** (C20).
47. Three failed logins **lock the account**; two failed security-question attempts lock it harder — requiring the TTB Helpdesk (7 AM-8 PM ET weekdays) (C17, C18).
48. Stale browser caches showing the wrong label image are a known issue with **official cache-clearing instructions per browser** in the FAQ (C35).

## E. Filing workflow and statuses

49. The eApplication lifecycle statuses are: **Received → Approved / Conditionally Approved / Needs Correction / Rejected** (C22).
50. **"Needs Correction"** is an intermediate status *unavailable to paper filers* — TTB returns the application with a list of required corrections (C23).
51. The correction window is **30 calendar days**; miss it and the system **automatically rejects** the application (C3, C23).
52. **Corrected applications get priority** — they re-enter the queue ahead of un-touched "Received" applications (C3).
53. A rejected label may be **resubmitted as a new application** — but without priority (C23).
54. "Rejected" is TTB's **final decision** status; paper applications can go straight there with no correction step (C23).
55. Status visibility is limited to **your own eApplications** — nothing about other companies, nothing about your own paper filings (C22).
56. Status-change **emails go to the submitter** — so when a third-party filer submits, the *filer*, not the brand owner, gets the notifications (C22).
57. Applicants can search all their own eApplications and statuses in-system (C22).
58. TTB posts **current label processing times publicly** so applicants don't need to call (C6).
59. Recent median processing times: **wine 4 days, spirits 6 days, malt beverage 1 day** — medians, meaning half take longer (processing-times page).
60. Historic guidance put typical turnaround at **5-20 days** depending on commodity and season (vendor/industry sources).
61. TTB has issued **over 2.9 million COLAs electronically**, at a rate of roughly **2,500 approvals per week** (registry statistics).
62. The human-review step is retained by design; e-filing's speedup comes from eliminating mail time, input validation, and the correction loop (C4).

## F. Attachments and label images (directly relevant to any OCR pipeline)

63. Label images must be **JPEG (.jpg/.jpeg) or PNG (.png)** — TIFF is rejected with an error (C28).
64. Supporting attachments may be **DOC/DOCX, TXT, PDF, JPEG** (C29).
65. Up to **10 attachment files** per application, each capped at **750KB** (C25).
66. Label image files are capped at **1.5MB each** (C30).
67. TTB's recommended resolution: **120-170 dpi** — chosen so "label specialists can read every word" (C30).
68. Recommended JPEG compression: **"Medium" quality (≈7/10)** (C30).
69. **Each label panel (brand, back, neck, etc.) must be uploaded as a separate file** (C31) — multi-image is the *norm* in the real system.
70. Images must be **cropped to the label's edges** — background/white space must be removed (C31).
71. Applicants must manually enter the **printed label's physical dimensions** in Step 3; the image's own dimensions may differ (C31).
72. **Wrong dimensions distort the printable version** used by specialists and published in the registry, and can delay approval (C31, C33).
73. Blurry text remediation advice: raise resolution toward 170 dpi, and if still poor, **switch to PNG** (C32).
74. Upload corruption ("glitches during data transfer") is acknowledged; the fix is re-save and re-upload, verified via a **"Verify Uploaded Images"** step (C34).
75. Lab analyses and organic certificates are uploaded as attachments in Step 3 (C25).
76. The printable version (application + images at stated dimensions) is **what the specialist actually reviews** (C33).

## G. Formulas Online (the sibling system)

77. Some products need **formula approval before label approval** — handled in the separate Formulas Online system (C24, C36).
78. Electronic formula and **pre-import** applications have been available since **January 2011** (C24).
79. One user ID spans both systems, but Formulas access must be **explicitly added** to a COLAs Online profile — it is "not automatic and registration is not instantaneous" (C36, C38).
80. Formulas Online includes **step-by-step guidance and validation checks** analogous to COLAs Online's (C36).

## H. Allowable revisions (change without a new COLA)

81. Once approved, a label may be changed in specific ways **without any new COLA** — the list lives on **pages 3-4 of Form 5100.31 itself** (allowable-revisions page).
82. The list runs to roughly **two dozen numbered items**, each with conditions in a "Comments" column.
83. Allowed: **deleting non-mandatory information** — e.g., removing an illustration or a fanciful name (item 1).
84. Allowed: **changing the label's shape, background color, and fonts** (item 3) — meaning the *same COLA* can look visually very different on shelf.
85. Allowed: **adding or changing a vintage date** on wine (item 5).
86. Allowed: **changing the alcohol content statement** (item 11) — within the regulatory bounds for the product class.
87. Allowed: **changing the net contents statement** (item 10).
88. Allowed: swapping bottling-statement phrasing, e.g., **"Produced by" → "Vinted by"** (item 6).
89. Allowed: **changing the address within the same state** (item 19).
90. Allowed: **adding a QR code** (item 23) and **adding a website address** (item 24).
91. All revisions must still comply with **27 CFR parts 4, 5, 7, 16** — allowable ≠ unregulated.
92. TTB provides an **"Allowable Changes Sample Label Generator"** tool illustrating worked examples — the agency's own visual-diff teaching aid.
93. Deep implication for verification tooling: **the label on a bottle may legitimately differ from the label in the registry** across shape, color, fonts, ABV statement, net contents, address, and added elements — field-by-field comparison with per-field rules is the only sound model (exactly the take-home's design).

## I. Systemic facts worth holding

94. The correction loop (C3/C23) means TTB's *own* workflow is "flag → human fixes → priority re-review" — the same triage architecture a screening assistant automates one step earlier.
95. The 30-day auto-rejection is the system's only hard automation of a *decision* — and it is a timeout, not a judgment.
96. TTB's imaging guidance (120-170 dpi, medium JPEG, cropped, per-panel files) effectively defines the **expected input distribution for any OCR pipeline** built against registry/label data.
97. The per-panel upload rule (fact 69) confirms that mandatory information is **distributed across multiple images** in real filings — single-image verification is a stated simplification, never an assumption to hide.
98. The signature-authority chain (facts 28-32) means any submission-side automation must sit **behind an authorized human filer** — there is no lawful "robot submits" path; screening-not-approval is structural, not stylistic.
99. TTB publishes its own **inconsistency admission** (fact 21) — the agency itself frames human variance as the residual problem, which is the strongest official warrant for consistency-focused tooling.
100. Everything above sits behind a **zero-fee, public, paper-compatible** front door (facts 7, 8, 13) — any modernization must preserve that equity floor, which a free advisory pre-screen does and a paid gatekeeper would not.
